### 6 September 2026 — choosing and refining the argument

I first considered arguing that AI is not capable of establishing new algorithms. After checking research on AI-generated ideas, I rejected that wording because it was too absolute. An AI system may generate an idea that appears novel, while still being unreliable at evaluating its feasibility, originality, or contribution. My concern is therefore not whether AI can ever produce something new, but whether I can understand and verify what it produces.

I then compared several possible examples from my own practice and selected my final-year AIGC game project. I kept the distinction between generating an asset and building a playable system. It reflects my experience more accurately and connects my artistic practice directly to programming.

At this stage, I used AI to propose and critique an essay structure, but I did not adopt the complete draft. I chose to publish the structure first and to verify the ownership and details of each project contribution before writing the personal example.

## What I kept

I kept the argument that generative output becomes part of a creative work only after it has been directed, constrained, evaluated, and integrated into a functioning system. This position is specific to my game-production experience and explains why technical understanding matters to my intended role as a producer.

## What I rejected

I rejected the broad claim that AI is incapable of producing novel ideas. It was rhetorically convenient but not adequately supported. I also rejected using an immediately polished full essay before checking which pipeline decisions and implementations were individually mine and which were shared by the project team.

### 7 September 2026 — adding empathy and artistic intention

I expanded the argument from technical judgement to artistic responsibility. I kept the idea that AI can be a valuable collaborator rather than an opponent: it helps me explore materials and express intentions that would otherwise be difficult to prototype quickly.

I added empathy as a central part of my position. I do not claim that AI can never produce emotional-looking content. My argument is that I remain responsible for deciding what the game is trying to express, what human experience it represents, and how players are invited to feel. I connected this claim to my final-year AIGC game project and described my role as overall designer and producer, while keeping the distinction between my direction and shared technical implementation.
